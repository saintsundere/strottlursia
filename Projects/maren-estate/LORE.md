# The Lay of Hale
### the Drownsong ballad — canon, verses by Clarey
*Sung before it was ever played. The keystone under all eight legends.*

> Watcher of our vale was a pale lass named Hale
> Rose from the sea with legs and a tail
> Hale with a gift of keening to the wight
> Doused our doelings and billies with sleep through the night
>
> But through generations did memory water down
> As did our thanks for Hale’s reach for our town
> Crossing of Crossings our vale did then name
> When one of Four Fords doom a whole land to shame
>
> *On this Drownsong, plucked record of old
> Keening croon wail, so above as so below*
>
> Cross of the cross planned for the Fifteenth of March
> Months of meeting in cloak and dagger dark
> Billieux, Bellwether, Bel and Bil “us” too
> Noble tithes paid with red not inherited blue
>
> On a peas harvest, Hale descended from the Mount
> To dance leg and tail and all our blessings she’d count
> In mid-dance, our lass Hale while her belly held two
> By Bel-Bil-Loo-Weather-ous was our watcher ran through
>
> *For the Drownsong, Thrum fibers did graft
> Opposite spirals foretell our Future from the Past*
>
> When Hale was felled, her body frothed into foam
> A rainbow in every bubble did her death bless the loam
> Her red locks shredded thinner and smaller in rain
> And spread o’er the vale where her body was slain
>
> Trapped ‘tween ‘bove and ‘low’s eternal schemes
> Half born with no mother to hold them, only ravine
> Their cries echo o’er our long unwatched vale
> Carrion birthed from the red hair of sea gravid Hale

---

## What the Lay canonizes (working notes)

- **Elah is Hale reversed** — and *Bellwether* is named among the conspirator
  families. Elah Bellwether carried the name of her watcher's killers into the
  house that collects the debt.
- **Thrum fibers grafted the Drownsong** — Thrum ↔ **Thrushwick**. Corwen's blood
  may be woven into this too. The house did not take two random students.
- **The vale is *unwatched*.** The land's "adaptations" are not an immune system
  that happened — they are the debris and half-born grief of a murdered guardian:
  her shredded red hair (the Greywitch broods; the red of Ruby Hills), her
  half-born twins (Lanterna's Rest — trapped between above and below, like an
  upside-down tree), her flock-blessings (the Doeling?), her keening (the
  Drownsong — a lullaby with no singer left, pulling sleepers down).
- **The keeper's whistle is a cover version of Hale's keening.** The Marens never
  invented dormancy; they imitate her lullaby, one note at a time.
- **"So above as so below"** — the mirror-water law: the reflecting pond, the
  upside-down tree, the shell mirror, the Drownsong's opposite spirals that
  foretell the Future from the Past.
- **The Fifteenth of March.** The Cross of the Cross — the anniversary. If the
  game takes place on **May 15** (graduation weeks away), then "missing 61 days"
  means Elah and Corwen entered the estate **on the Ides — the anniversary of
  Hale's murder.** (March 15 + 61 days = May 15, exactly.)
- **The Drownsong is an object**: a *plucked* record of old — spiral-strung with
  grafted Thrum fiber, replaying the keening. It lives in (or under) the chapel;
  the mute bell was its answer, clapper since removed.
- **The Chapel's win condition is not taking — it is answering.** The keening
  leaves spaces where a reply should be. The room is finished when the song is
  answered — and the music that remains is the jazz rendition: two voices
  improvising to each other. A hymn that learned to answer itself.

## Open questions for Clarey
1. Where do the **Marens** stand in the Lay? Proposal: they were the one Ford
   family that refused the Cross — stayed ferrymen, stayed unennobled ("red not
   inherited blue" bought the others' titles), and took up keeping as the debt
   the guilty would not pay. Widowmere: the mere widowed of its watcher.
2. Are the Lanterna twins **the** half-born two? (Assumed yes.)
3. The Doeling of Saywardbury — one of Hale's blessings gone feral, or something
   that came *out of the ravine*?
